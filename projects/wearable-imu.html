IMU-based movement assessment

Turning raw wrist and hip inertial signals into the numbers a coach or clinician needs: how many repetitions, through what range of motion, and how fast.

This is industry work with proprietary data and code. To respect that, this page describes the method only. It shows no recordings, accuracy figures or plots of real data. The diagram below is a schematic.

The problem

Wearable training suits record motion continuously, but users need interpretable outputs: a repetition count, the range of motion of each repetition, and its mean and peak velocity. These have to be robust to sensors placed at different body sites, unsynchronised streams, dropped samples and very different exercise styles. They must also run on a phone.

My contribution

  • Synchronisation and data quality. Aligned accelerometer and gyroscope streams from wrist and hip sensors by timestamp, and checked for missing samples before any model evaluation.
  • Signal processing. Filtering, FFT analysis, windowing, peak detection and temporal segmentation to isolate movement cycles from accelerometer, gyroscope and quaternion signals.
  • Models. Regression and classification models that count repetitions and estimate range of motion, mean velocity and peak velocity.
  • Validation and deployment. Compared estimates against experimental reference data, refined the models based on their errors, and exported lightweight models for mobile and embedded inference.

Pipeline

Why this matters for biomedical engineering

The same chain of synchronisation, artefact handling, cycle segmentation and validation against a reference applies directly to clinical gait analysis, rehabilitation monitoring and wearable ECG or EEG. It complements my academic instrumentation work on an EEG front-end and the CardioNex ECG garment.
